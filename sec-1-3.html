    Section 1.3 Intercepts

    In this Lesson we find the intercepts of a linear model, and interpret their meaning in the context of the model. Some students will have forgotten how to find the intercepts, and most will need help interpreting the meaning of the intercepts in context. A common mistake is to give the meaning of the variable on the appropriate axis, instead of its value at the intercept.
    We also review the intercept method for graphing a line. Finally, we consider linear models given in the general linear form, \(~Ax+By=C\text{.}\)

    Activity 1.3.2. General Form for a Linear Equation, \(~Ax + By = C\).

    The owner of a gas station has $19,200 to spend on unleaded gas this month. Regular unleaded costs him $2.40 per gallon, and premium unleaded costs $3.20 per gallon.
    1. Write algebraic expressions to answer the questions:
      1a. Many students will immediately write an equation (the answer to part (b)), but will have trouble answering the two questions in part (a).
      How much do \(x\) gallons of regular cost?
      How much do \(y\) gallons of premium cost?

    Subsection 1.3.1 Check Your Understanding

    1. In Activity 1, how did you use algebra to find the intercepts of the graph?
    2. In Activity 1, which intercept gives the starting value of \(C\text{?}\)
    3. The equation you wrote in Activity 2 was different in form from the equation you wrote in Activity 1. How?
    4. In Activity 2, what did the variables \(x\) and \(y\) represent?

    Subsection 1.3.2 Wrap-Up

    In this Lesson, we worked on the following skills and goals related to linear models:
    • Find the intercepts of the graph of a linear equation.
    • Interpret the intercepts of the graph of a linear model.
    • Write a model in general linear form, \(Ax+By=C\text{.}\)
    • Graph a line by the intercept method.
    • Solve a linear equation for \(y\) in terms of \(x\text{.}\)

    Subsection 1.3.3 Questions for Writing or Discussion

    1. Explain how to graph a line by the intercept method.
    2. Explain how the words intercept and intersect are related, and how they are different.
    3. In what situations is the general linear form easier to use for a model than the form \(y = \text{(starting value)} + \text{rate} \cdot x\text{?}\)
    4. Explain why setting \(y=0\) in a linear equation allows us to find the \(x\)-intercept.

    Concept Questions.

    1. Suppose a line is increasing and its \(y\)-intercept is positive. Which is true:
      1. Its \(x\)-intercept is positive.
      2. Its \(x\)-intercept is negative.
      3. We can’t tell.
      4. The line may not have an \(x\)-intercept.
    2. Is it possible for the \(x\)-intercept and the \(y\)-intercept of a line to be the same point?
      1. No
      2. Yes
      3. Only for a vertical line
      4. They are always the same point.
    3. What is the \(y\)-coordinate of any point on the \(x\)-axis?
      1. \(\displaystyle (x,y)\)
      2. \(\displaystyle Y\)
      3. It depends on the value of \(x\text{.}\)
      4. \(\displaystyle 0\)
    4. Delbert says that the intercepts of the line \(~3x+5y=30~\) are \((10,6)\text{.}\) What is wrong with his answer?
      1. \((10,6)\) is not on the \(x\)-axis.
      2. An intercept must have a \(0\) coordinate.
      3. The line has two intercepts.
      4. All of the above.
